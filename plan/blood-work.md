# Blood Work

When you get your results, paste them in or tell Claude the numbers, and they'll be recorded here. Then the nutrition plan gets adjusted to match. The plan to retest after about 12 weeks of the new routine (around late December 2026) lets you see whether the changes are working.

> The reference ranges below are general adult guidelines. Your doctor sets your actual targets based on your overall heart-disease risk, which also depends on age, blood pressure, family history, smoking, and diabetes. Go by your doctor's targets when they differ from these.

## Questions worth asking your doctor

1. **What is my 10-year cardiovascular risk score?** This calculation, often called the ASCVD or PREVENT score, is what most doctors use to decide whether diet alone is enough or medication should be considered.
2. **Should I get a coronary artery calcium (CAC) scan?** It's a quick, low-cost CT scan that shows whether plaque has built up in your heart arteries. For people in their late 50s and early 60s with borderline cholesterol, it often answers the question "do I need a statin?" more clearly than cholesterol numbers alone.
3. **Has my Lp(a) ever been checked?** It's a genetic risk factor that diet doesn't change. It only needs to be measured once, and many people have never had it done.
4. **Is ApoB worth adding to my next panel?** Many cardiologists consider it a more accurate measure of risk than LDL.
5. **Any concerns about me taking creatine or psyllium fiber?** Both are discussed in this plan.
