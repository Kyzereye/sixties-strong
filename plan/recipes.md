# Starter Recipes

These six recipes build on what you already like to eat: chicken and rice, soup, beef, pasta, beans, and eggs. Each is adjusted to be higher in protein, higher in fiber, and lower in saturated fat. Four of them make several servings, so one cooking session covers lunches or dinners for most of a week. Three use the slow cooker.

The nutrition numbers are **estimates per serving**, worked out from standard values for each ingredient. They'll be close enough for tracking. Brands vary, so if a label says something different, the label wins.

**Why these four numbers?** Calories drive fat loss. Protein builds and keeps muscle. Fiber, especially the soluble kind in oats, beans, lentils, and barley, directly lowers LDL cholesterol. Saturated fat is the part of your diet that raises LDL the most. Your daily targets are about **2,100 calories, 150+ g protein, 30+ g fiber, and under 15 g saturated fat.**

## 1. Slow-Cooker Chicken and White Bean Chili

This is the backbone recipe. It's cheap, it freezes well, and a single bowl gives you almost a third of your daily protein and a big dose of fiber. It's a lighter "white" chili with no beef and no cheese stirred in, but it's still rich and filling thanks to the beans and spices.

**Makes:** 6 servings, about 2 cups each
**Prep:** 15 minutes | **Cook:** 6–8 hours on low, or 3–4 hours on high

### Ingredients

- 2 lb boneless, skinless chicken breasts (or trimmed thighs; see variations)
- 3 cans (15 oz each) cannellini or great northern beans, drained and rinsed
- 1½ cups frozen corn
- 1 large onion, diced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 cans (4 oz each) diced mild green chiles, not drained
- 4 cups low-sodium chicken broth
- 2 teaspoons ground cumin
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano
- 1 teaspoon chili powder
- ½ teaspoon black pepper
- ½ teaspoon salt (add more at the end only if it needs it)
- Juice of 1 lime
- ½ cup chopped fresh cilantro (optional)
- For serving (optional): 2 tablespoons nonfat plain Greek yogurt per bowl, in place of sour cream

### Steps

1. Put the onion, garlic, green chiles, corn, and two of the three cans of beans in the slow cooker.
2. Put the third can of beans in a bowl and mash it roughly with a fork or potato masher, then add it to the cooker. The mashed beans thicken the chili naturally without cream or flour.
3. Sprinkle in the cumin, oregano, chili powder, pepper, and salt, and stir.
4. Lay the chicken breasts on top in a single layer and pour the broth over everything.
5. Cover and cook on low for 6 to 8 hours or on high for 3 to 4 hours, until the chicken shreds easily with a fork.
6. Take the chicken out, shred it with two forks, and stir it back in.
7. Stir in the lime juice and cilantro. Taste, and add salt only if it needs it. The lime brightens the flavor enough that it usually doesn't.

### Per serving (about 2 cups)

**~420 calories · 48 g protein · 11 g fiber · 1 g saturated fat**

### Storage and reheating

The chili keeps in the fridge for 4 days and in the freezer for 3 months. Freeze it in single-serving containers so you always have a 5-minute meal ready, which beats a drive-thru on a busy day. It thickens in the fridge, so add a splash of water or broth when reheating.

### Variations

- **Thighs instead of breasts:** juicier and more forgiving, adding about 40 calories and 1.5 g of saturated fat per serving. Trim off the visible fat.
- **More heat:** add a diced jalapeño at the start or a pinch of cayenne at the end.
- **Over rice:** serve a smaller bowl (1½ cups) over ½ cup of brown rice on a big ride day.

## 2. Slow-Cooker Lentil and Vegetable Soup

This is your homemade replacement for canned soup. A typical can of soup has 1,500 to 1,800 mg of sodium. This version has well under half that per bowl, and it costs less per serving than most canned soups. Lentils are one of the best foods you can eat for cholesterol because they're packed with soluble fiber.

On its own, the soup is a bit light on protein for a meal, so the recipe adds shredded chicken. A store rotisserie chicken with the skin removed is the easy way to do it.

**Makes:** 6 servings, about 2 cups each
**Prep:** 15 minutes | **Cook:** 7–8 hours on low, or 4 hours on high

### Ingredients

- 2 cups dry brown or green lentils, rinsed and picked over for small stones
- 1 large onion, diced
- 3 carrots, peeled and diced
- 3 stalks celery, diced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 can (14.5 oz) no-salt-added diced tomatoes, with the juice
- 8 cups low-sodium chicken or vegetable broth
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 teaspoon ground cumin
- 1 teaspoon dried thyme
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika
- ½ teaspoon black pepper
- ½ teaspoon salt (adjust at the end)
- 4 cups fresh spinach or chopped kale
- 2 cups shredded cooked chicken, skin removed (about 10 oz, from a rotisserie chicken or leftovers)
- 1 tablespoon red wine vinegar or lemon juice

### Steps

1. Put the lentils, onion, carrots, celery, garlic, tomatoes, olive oil, bay leaves, cumin, thyme, smoked paprika, pepper, and salt in the slow cooker.
2. Pour in the broth and stir.
3. Cover and cook on low for 7 to 8 hours or on high for about 4 hours, until the lentils are completely tender.
4. Remove the bay leaves. Stir in the spinach and shredded chicken, and let it sit for 10 minutes until the greens wilt and the chicken heats through.
5. Stir in the vinegar or lemon juice. That splash of acid at the end makes the soup taste brighter and less flat, so you need less salt. Taste and adjust.
6. **Optional:** for a thicker, creamier soup, blend about a quarter of it with an immersion blender before adding the chicken.

### Per serving (about 2 cups)

**~380 calories · 32 g protein · 13 g fiber · 1.5 g saturated fat**
*(Without the chicken: about 300 calories, 19 g protein, 13 g fiber, under 1 g saturated fat.)*

### Storage and reheating

The soup keeps in the fridge for 5 days and in the freezer for 3 months. Lentils soak up liquid as they sit, so add broth or water when reheating.

### Variations

- **Smoky:** add 8 oz of diced lean turkey kielbasa in place of the chicken for a smoky, ham-and-bean-soup flavor. Turkey kielbasa is saltier, so skip the added salt.
- **Split pea:** use green split peas instead of lentils and add an extra cup of broth.
- **Italian:** swap the cumin and paprika for 2 teaspoons of Italian seasoning, then top each bowl with a teaspoon of grated Parmesan.

## 3. Lemon-Garlic Chicken with Brown Rice and Peas

This is your everyday chicken, rice, and peas, made better. The marinade adds a lot of flavor with almost no extra calories. Brown rice brings more fiber than white rice. The portion is a full, satisfying plate, with about 6 oz of cooked chicken per serving. It's built to be cooked once on a Sunday and eaten as lunches and dinners through the week.

**Makes:** 4 servings
**Prep:** 10 minutes, plus at least 30 minutes to marinate | **Cook:** about 25 minutes, plus rice time

### Ingredients

**Chicken:**

- 2 lb boneless, skinless chicken breasts
- 2 tablespoons olive oil
- Zest and juice of 2 lemons
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano
- 1 teaspoon paprika
- ½ teaspoon salt
- ½ teaspoon black pepper

**Sides:**

- 4 cups cooked brown rice (from 1⅓ cups dry, or 4 single-cup microwave pouches)
- 2 cups frozen peas (or 1 cup peas + 1 cup corn)
- Optional: 1 cup steamed broccoli per serving (adds about 55 calories, 4 g protein, 5 g fiber)

### Steps

1. **Even out the chicken.** If the breasts are thick at one end, put them in a zip-top bag and pound the thick end with a rolling pin or the bottom of a pan until they're an even ¾ inch thick. This is the secret to chicken breast that isn't dry, because the thin end isn't overcooked while the thick end catches up.
2. **Marinate.** In a bowl or zip-top bag, mix the olive oil, lemon zest and juice, garlic, oregano, paprika, salt, and pepper. Add the chicken and coat it. Marinate for at least 30 minutes, or up to overnight in the fridge. Don't go longer than that, because the lemon starts to make the texture mushy.
3. **Start the rice.** If you're cooking rice from scratch, combine 1⅓ cups brown rice with 2⅔ cups water and a pinch of salt. Bring to a boil, cover, and simmer on low for 40 to 45 minutes. Then turn off the heat and let it sit, still covered, for 10 minutes. A rice cooker works too, or use microwave pouches.
4. **Cook the chicken.** Pick one method:
   - **Oven:** heat to 425°F. Put the chicken on a lined sheet pan and bake for 18 to 22 minutes.
   - **Skillet:** heat a large skillet over medium-high and cook the chicken for 5 to 7 minutes per side.
   - **Grill:** medium-high heat, 5 to 6 minutes per side.

   Whichever you choose, the chicken is done at **165°F** inside. A cheap instant-read thermometer takes the guessing out of chicken, and it's the best $15 you'll spend in the kitchen.
5. **Rest, then slice.** Let the chicken rest for 5 minutes before cutting so the juices stay in.
6. **Cook the peas.** Microwave them with a splash of water for 3 to 4 minutes, or stir them into the hot rice for the last 5 minutes of its resting time.
7. **Portion it out.** Divide the chicken, rice, and peas into 4 containers, about 1 cup of rice, ½ cup of peas, and a quarter of the chicken in each.

### Per serving

**~620 calories · 60 g protein · 8 g fiber · 2.5 g saturated fat**

### Storage and reheating

Keeps in the fridge for 4 days. Sprinkle a few drops of water over the rice before microwaving so it doesn't dry out.

### Variations

- **Lighter plate:** use ¾ cup rice and add a cup of broccoli for about 570 calories with more fiber.
- **Southwest:** swap the lemon and oregano for lime juice, cumin, and chili powder, and use black beans in place of half the peas.
- **Sandwich use:** slice a leftover breast thin for sandwiches. It's far better for you than deli ham.

## 4. Slow-Cooker Beef and Barley Stew

You like beef, and you like the slow cooker in winter, so this is the recipe for that. It uses **round**, a lean cut that becomes tender when cooked low and slow, instead of fatty chuck. That keeps saturated fat low. Barley is one of the few grains with as much cholesterol-lowering soluble fiber as oats, and it makes the stew thick and hearty.

**Makes:** 6 servings, about 2 cups each
**Prep:** 25 minutes | **Cook:** 8 hours on low

### Ingredients

- 2 lb beef bottom round or eye of round, trimmed of all visible fat and cut into 1-inch cubes
- 2 tablespoons flour
- ½ teaspoon salt and ½ teaspoon black pepper
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 large onion, diced
- 4 carrots, peeled and cut into 1-inch chunks
- 3 stalks celery, sliced
- 8 oz mushrooms, sliced
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- ¾ cup pearl barley, rinsed
- 2 tablespoons tomato paste
- 1 tablespoon Worcestershire sauce
- 6 cups low-sodium beef broth
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 teaspoon dried thyme
- ½ teaspoon dried rosemary
- 2 cups frozen cut green beans (or peas)

### Steps

1. **Coat the beef.** Toss the beef cubes with the flour, salt, and pepper.
2. **Brown it.** Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high. Brown the beef in two batches, about 2 minutes per side, without crowding the pan. Browning creates most of the deep, beefy flavor in the finished stew, so don't skip it. Move the browned beef to the slow cooker.
3. **Scrape up the flavor.** Pour about ½ cup of the broth into the hot skillet and scrape up the browned bits on the bottom with a wooden spoon. Pour all of it into the slow cooker.
4. **Load the slow cooker.** Add the onion, carrots, celery, mushrooms, garlic, barley, tomato paste, Worcestershire, bay leaves, thyme, rosemary, and the rest of the broth. Stir to dissolve the tomato paste.
5. **Cook.** Cover and cook on low for 8 hours, until the beef is fork-tender and the barley is soft. Lean round needs the full low-and-slow time to become tender, so don't rush it on high.
6. **Finish.** Stir in the frozen green beans for the last 30 minutes, or just stir them in at the end and let the heat of the stew cook them through in about 10 minutes. Remove the bay leaves.
7. Taste and adjust the salt and pepper.

### Per serving (about 2 cups)

**~380 calories · 42 g protein · 7 g fiber · 2 g saturated fat**

### Storage and reheating

Keeps in the fridge for 4 days and freezes well for 3 months. The barley keeps soaking up liquid, so the stew will be very thick the next day. Add broth or water when reheating to loosen it.

### Variations

- **Chuck roast instead of round:** more marbled, more tender, and more forgiving, but it adds about 3 to 4 g of saturated fat per serving. Fine occasionally. Trim it well.
- **With potatoes:** add 1 lb of diced Yukon gold potatoes and reduce the barley to ½ cup. Good on a big ride day.
- **Beef and bean:** replace half the beef with 2 cans of rinsed kidney beans. It's cheaper, it adds more fiber, and the beef flavor is still there.

## 5. Turkey Meatballs with Whole-Wheat Pasta and Marinara

Pasta night stays. The changes are lean turkey meatballs made with oats, which add soluble fiber and keep them moist, whole-wheat pasta for more fiber and protein than white, and a measured 2-oz portion of pasta. That's the standard serving, and it's smaller than most people eyeball. The meatballs freeze beautifully, so make a double batch.

**Makes:** 4 servings (about 20 meatballs)
**Prep:** 20 minutes | **Cook:** 20 minutes

### Ingredients

**Meatballs:**

- 1¼ lb (20 oz) 93% lean ground turkey
- ½ cup quick or old-fashioned oats
- 1 large egg
- ¼ cup grated Parmesan cheese
- ¼ cup finely grated or minced onion
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- ¼ cup chopped fresh parsley (or 1 tablespoon dried)
- 1 teaspoon Italian seasoning
- ½ teaspoon salt
- ¼ teaspoon black pepper
- ¼ teaspoon red pepper flakes (optional)

**Pasta and sauce:**

- 8 oz dry whole-wheat spaghetti or penne
- 1 jar (24 oz) marinara sauce. Look for one with no added sugar and under 400 mg of sodium per ½ cup.

### Steps

1. Heat the oven to 400°F and line a sheet pan with foil or parchment.
2. In a large bowl, mix the oats, egg, Parmesan, onion, garlic, parsley, Italian seasoning, salt, pepper, and red pepper flakes. Let it sit for 2 minutes so the oats soften.
3. Add the turkey and mix gently with your hands until just combined. Overmixing makes meatballs tough.
4. With wet hands, roll the mixture into about 20 meatballs, a little smaller than a golf ball, and set them on the sheet pan.
5. Bake for 18 to 20 minutes, until they're browned and reach **165°F** inside.
6. While the meatballs bake, cook the pasta in salted boiling water according to the package directions. Drain.
7. Warm the marinara in a large pan. Add the baked meatballs and simmer for 5 minutes so they absorb some sauce.
8. Divide the pasta into 4 portions, about 1 cup cooked each, and top each with 5 meatballs and a quarter of the sauce.

**Slow-cooker option:** bake the meatballs as above, then put them in the slow cooker with the sauce on low for 3 to 4 hours. Cook the pasta just before serving.

### Per serving (1 cup pasta, 5 meatballs, sauce)

**~595 calories · 41 g protein · 11 g fiber · 5 g saturated fat**

Add a side salad (2 cups of greens with cucumber, tomato, and 1 tablespoon of light vinaigrette, about 60 calories) or a cup of roasted broccoli to round out the meal.

### Storage and reheating

Cooked meatballs keep in the fridge for 4 days or the freezer for 3 months. Store pasta separately from the sauce and meatballs if you can, so it doesn't turn soft.

### Variations

- **Lower saturated fat:** use 99% lean ground turkey breast and cut the Parmesan to 2 tablespoons. That drops it to about 3 g of saturated fat per serving. The meatballs will be a little less juicy.
- **Higher protein pasta:** chickpea or lentil pasta has nearly twice the protein of regular pasta and even more fiber.
- **Meatball subs:** 3 meatballs and sauce on a small whole-wheat roll with a sprinkle of part-skim mozzarella.

## 6. Black Bean Breakfast Skillet

This is a fast, high-protein breakfast that keeps you full until lunch. It uses one whole egg plus egg whites. That gives you the flavor and nutrients of the yolk while keeping cholesterol and saturated fat in check until we see your blood work. The black beans add fiber, which is rare in a typical breakfast.

**Makes:** 1 serving (scale up as needed)
**Prep:** 5 minutes | **Cook:** 8 minutes

### Ingredients

- 1 teaspoon olive oil (or cooking spray)
- ¼ cup diced onion
- ½ cup diced bell pepper (any color)
- 1 handful (about 1 cup) fresh spinach
- ½ cup canned black beans, drained and rinsed
- 1 large egg
- ¾ cup liquid egg whites (from a carton)
- Pinch of salt, black pepper, and ground cumin
- 2 tablespoons shredded reduced-fat cheddar
- 2 tablespoons salsa
- 1 slice whole-wheat toast or 1 small whole-wheat tortilla

### Steps

1. Heat the oil in a nonstick skillet over medium heat. Add the onion and bell pepper and cook for 3 to 4 minutes, until they soften.
2. Add the black beans and cumin and stir for a minute to warm them through.
3. Add the spinach and stir until it wilts, about 30 seconds.
4. In a small bowl, whisk the egg, egg whites, salt, and pepper together. Pour into the skillet.
5. Let it set for about 20 seconds, then gently push the eggs around with a spatula, folding them over until just cooked, about 2 to 3 minutes. Pull them off the heat while they're still slightly glossy, because they keep cooking on the plate.
6. Top with the cheese and salsa. Serve with the toast, or wrap it all in the tortilla to eat on the go.

### Per serving

**~515 calories · 43 g protein · 13 g fiber · 4.5 g saturated fat**

### Make-ahead version

Sauté a big batch of the onion, peppers, and beans on Sunday and keep it in the fridge. Each morning, warm a scoop and scramble the eggs into it. Breakfast takes about 4 minutes.

### Variations

- **If your blood work looks good:** use 2 whole eggs and ½ cup of whites. Adds about 50 calories and 1.5 g of saturated fat.
- **Avocado instead of cheese:** ¼ avocado adds healthy fats and 2.5 g of fiber with less saturated fat.
- **Leftover chicken:** toss in 2 oz of diced cooked chicken and cut back on the egg whites.
